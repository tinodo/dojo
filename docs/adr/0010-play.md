# ADR 0010 - Play: mastery moments, a weekly rhythm, study points, and learning together without a ranking

- Status: proposed (design only; no application code changed). Revised after the design review (GPT
  "rework", Gemini "approve with fixes") and two fix checks, with the lead's decisions of 2026-10-02. Play builds on two
  features still in progress: Know what you know (ADR 0011, `feature/know`) and Team Dojo (ADR 0009,
  `feature/team`). **The social layer is gated exactly as Team Dojo is:** it exists only in team mode, and
  team mode is not switched on for real learners until the accountable privacy owner outside the build team
  and, for colleagues in Germany or the Netherlands, the works councils have accepted `docs/privacy.md`
  (with Play's section) in writing (ADR 0009, "Operator access and the residual risk").
- Date: 2026-10-01 (revised 2026-10-02)
- Decision owner: the owner. Only the owner can switch on the social layer (it also needs team mode, which
  only he can switch on, behind the gate above). The personal layer is each learner's own choice.
- Decision: proposed by the Play builder. The lead may reject or change it in review. The review triggers
  are below.
- Guardrails: EG-12 (record consequential decisions), EG-16 (observations are not findings), EG-21
  (product telemetry is not learner evidence), EG-24 (ownership and isolation in the data model; tests use
  two learners; no unrequested dashboard; minimum group sizes before any aggregate), EG-25 (disclosure is
  explicit, scoped and previewed; a favourable finding keeps its limits), EG-26 (deletion is real; retention
  by data class), EG-29 (observability is not surveillance), EG-30 (accessibility changes the experience,
  not status), EG-32 (claims match their evidence), EG-37 (retrieved content is untrusted), EG-43 (no new
  dependency); the Interaction-to-Evidence contract (pips: with help, on your own, later, in a new
  situation; multiple choice never fills a pip; a missing event is never read in the learner's favour; no
  pass prediction); ADR 0009 (members, roles, display names, leave) and ADR 0011 (due recalls, relearned).
- Vision: section II.6 "The social and emotional dimension", in full; "Learning with peers, without a
  performance dashboard" (II.7); the retirement of **Belt** (II.1); the Capability Record definition (II.2,
  "Not a badge, permission, or certificate"); INV-4 (currency without a universal expiry clock), INV-10
  (the learner is a principal), INV-16 (the record belongs to the learner; "privacy-preserving system
  calibration is distinct from a performance dashboard"), INV-18 (authority is named, never borrowed).
- Research: `research/2026-10-01-gamification.md` (the lead's commissioned report; numbers in brackets
  below refer to its sources), with `research/2026-10-01-learning-gaps.md` and
  `research/2026-10-01-multi-user-tech.md`.

## The requirement

The owner asked: "Perhaps there is a nice addition to add some gamification? People could either compete or have
some sort of ranking?"

The honest answer has two halves. Yes, some game elements help: on average a small to moderate effect.
No, a ranking of colleagues by score is the one thing Dojo should not build. This ADR records what Dojo
builds instead, the exact rules, and what may never be added later without a new ADR.

## What the research says

- **Small to moderate, and sometimes harmful.** Bias-corrected over ten meta-analyses: performance
  g = 0.32, motivation g = 0.18, with prediction intervals that include negative values: some designs make
  learning worse [4]. Cognitive outcomes g = 0.49 [1].
- **Relatedness moves most, competence least.** Relatedness g = 1.78, autonomy g = 0.64, competence only
  g = 0.28 [3]. **Competition combined with collaboration** works best for behaviour [1].
- **Informational, not controlling.** Expected rewards for doing or finishing a task lower free-choice
  motivation (d = -0.40 to -0.28); positive feedback raises it (d = +0.33) [8].
- **Leaderboards are the weakest element.** Badges plus a leaderboard lowered motivation and final-exam
  scores against a control over 16 weeks [9]. Points and leaderboards raise output, not motivation or
  perceived competence [10]. High-ranked people coast and take shortcuts [12]. Absolute boards discourage
  the bottom [30]. The effect comes from salient feedback, not the element [14]. Speed scoring cuts
  reflection and rewards guessing [15].
- **What drives learning** is spaced retrieval done on time (Cepeda 2006 [17]; Rowland 2014 [16];
  successive relearning, ADR 0011). So points reward that, not volume.
- **Law (not legal advice).** In Germany (BetrVG §87(1) no. 6, BAG 1 ABR 7/15) and the Netherlands (WOR
  art. 27(1)(l)), a system *objectively suitable* to monitor behaviour or performance needs the works
  council. Intent does not matter, and group data counts when the pressure passes to individuals. Consent
  between employer and employee is weak (GDPR Recital 43). A ranked board of colleagues is the feature most
  likely to fall under this [31]-[38].

## What Dojo's own documents require

- **Shame is the dropout mechanism** (II.6). Nothing in Play may tell a person, or their colleagues, that
  they are behind.
- **Two rooms.** A private room where ignorance is safe; a witness room where being seen is specific,
  ceremonial, and never feeds a manager. Play has both, on purpose: everything is private until the
  learner shares one thing, to a group they chose.
- **Peer participation is an invitation, never an assignment announcing who is weakest**; refusal is never
  recorded as avoidance (II.6, II.7).
- **A deficit-only ledger produces deficit-shaped people.** Mastery is marked and returnable-to on a bad
  week, not instantly converted into the next gap (II.6). Play's moments shelf is that.
- **No punished streak** (II.7, "A learner's journey"; ADR 0011 section 4).
- **Belt is retired:** coarse rank decoupled from ability (II.1). Play has no level, rank, tier, belt or XP.
  The board's weekly bands (section 5) describe one week's capped effort, carry no order and say nothing
  about ability.
- **The Capability Record is not a badge.** A moment is a marked event in the learner's own evidence, not a
  credential, and it says so wherever it is shown (INV-18).
- **The evidence model.** Points never fill a pip, never change a judgement, and never feed readiness, the
  plan or the next-step suggestions (EG-16, EG-21). Moments come only from what the evidence already shows.

## The options

1. **Do nothing.** Safe, but it ignores the strongest lever the research names (relatedness), leaves the
   vision's "hunger to be seen" unmet, and leaves mastery unmarked. Rejected.
2. **A ranked score leaderboard of colleagues** (by accuracy, practice-exam scores, readiness, skills shown
   or points without a cap). Rejected, for four independent reasons, any one of which is enough:
   - *Evidence:* the weakest element in the literature; it lowered motivation and exam scores [9], makes
     the top coast [12] and discourages the bottom [30]; ranking on ability works through competence, where
     gamification moves least [3].
   - *Vision:* it is a performance dashboard by another name (II.7), it announces who is weakest (II.6),
     and it is a coarse rank decoupled from ability, the very Belt the vision retired (II.1).
   - *Evidence model:* a score ranking would turn Dojo-only multiple-choice results into a public claim
     about people. INV-17 and the I2E contract forbid that use; the readiness page refuses even a private
     pass prediction.
   - *Law:* a ranked board of colleagues' results is objectively suitable to monitor performance. Among
     colleagues in Germany or the Netherlands it brings in the works council, whatever Dojo intends.
3. **XP, levels and badges as credentials** (the Microsoft Learn model). Rejected. A level is a rank; a
   badge for activity says nothing about capability and looks like a credential (II.2, INV-18). Points and
   levels raise output, not motivation [10].
4. **A daily streak with loss aversion.** Rejected. A broken streak "can feel quite demotivating" [21];
   the vision forbids punishing it, and ADR 0011 never counts missed days.
5. **A private layer that marks mastery and rewards effort and spacing, plus an opt-in social layer that is
   cooperative first, with one capped effort board as the only comparison** (chosen; ranked by expected
   value below).

## The decision

### 0. Rules for all of Play

- **Play never writes Dojo's evidence, and keeps no ledger of its own.** Points, the rhythm and moments are
  computed on the server from the learner's own Record each time they are viewed, by pure functions in
  `app/play.py`. Play stores only the learner's settings and choices (section 9), never a derived state
  that could drift from the Record. Play adds nothing to the Record,
  with two exceptions that are the learner's own learning data, not Play data: a duel's answers (section 6)
  and a reported pass (section 7).
- **Play never feeds evidence, readiness, the plan or the suggestions.** `evidence.derive`, `relearn`,
  `readiness`, `plan` and `suggestions` do not import `app/play.py`. Points are effort, not knowledge, and
  Dojo never turns them into a statement about motivation (EG-21).
- **Correctness never changes points, a rhythm day, or anything a peer sees** (except the two scores in a
  duel, which only the two players see). No rule pays for a miss or a success, now or later (section 3
  shows why). Confidence (ADR 0011) never changes anything either.
- **Nothing in Play is timed.** No speed, no per-question clock, no time ever shown to a peer. The only
  times Play uses are the Record's own (for days, weeks, the 5-second floor and expiry). Spoken and typed
  answers count the same (EG-30).
- **Days are calendar days in Europe/Berlin, and weeks start Monday 00:00 Europe/Berlin**, the same zone as
  the Plan and Know.
- **The words.** "Moments", "your week", "weeks met", "study points", "circle", "effort board", "duel".
  Never level, rank, tier, league, belt, XP, streak, "in a row", "behind", "missed", "lost", red, or a flame.
- **No notification ever** about a missed day, a week not met, a lower position, or an invitation not
  taken up. Play sends nothing outside the app today. If ADR 0013 adds push, Play may only send: an
  invitation to you, your circle meeting its goal, and a duel result that is ready.
- **The owner in Play.** The owner runs this Dojo (the operator, ADR 0009). In the app he is a learner like any
  other with three limits, because an invitation or a comparison from the person who runs the system is
  hard to refuse and easy to read as monitoring:
  - he **cannot join the effort board** (section 5);
  - he **may be in circles**, only when a member invites him; he sees the bar under the same 5+ rule as
    everyone and what members chose to share with that circle (section 4), nothing else; he does not
    start circles or invite to them;
  - he **may duel only when a member invites him**, and the member sees him labelled "runs this Dojo"
    (section 6).

  The owner's admin role adds nothing in Play: no admin route returns Play data (P-6). What he can reach
  as the operator, outside the app, is a separate matter, disclosed as ADR 0009 requires (P-12).

### 1. Mastery moments: a private shelf (single user; personal layer)

A moment is a dated event from the learner's own evidence that Dojo marks, so it can be returned to.

| Moment | Marked when | Date |
|---|---|---|
| Skill: on your own, later | The skill's "later" pip fills for the first time (an unaided answer, every point met, 20 hours or more after the last teaching, not disputed) | That answer |
| Skill: in a new situation | The "new situation" pip fills for the first time (scenario skills only) | That answer |
| Skill: relearned | Know's `relearned_at` (three on-time successes on three days since the last miss, ADR 0011) | That answer |
| Domain: every skill relearned | Every askable skill in the domain (it has a verified page and none is gone, ADR 0005/0006) has a relearned moment | The last of them |
| Exam: every domain relearned | Every domain of the exam has its domain moment | The last of them |
| Exam: passed | The learner reports a pass (section 7) | The date they give |

- **Nothing smaller is a moment.** "With help" and "on your own right after teaching" are progress, not
  mastery; multiple choice, practice exams, lessons and points never make a moment. A moment can only come
  from what the pips and Know already show, so it can never claim more than the evidence.
- **Derived at read time, never stored.** Each time the shelf is viewed, `play.moments` runs
  `evidence.derive` and Know's `relearn.schedule` over the learner's current Record and the current
  package, and lists every moment whose support holds now. Play stores only two things about moments: the
  learner's deletions (moment ids, so a deleted moment stays hidden; Settings can show it again) and the
  copies the learner shared to a circle (section 4). There is no stored shelf that could outlive its
  evidence.
- **Every supporting condition is re-checked, every time.** A moment holds only while every answer it
  rests on still qualifies under today's rules: for a pip moment, the answer that first filled it (not
  disputed, unaided, every point met, the timing rule of the pip); for a relearned moment, **all three**
  on-time successes, re-derived by Know's schedule from the whole sequence of attempts (a dispute or regrade
  of the first one changes the sequence, so it is caught, not only the last); for a domain moment, the
  relearned moment of every askable skill in the domain now (it has a verified page and is not gone, ADR
  0005/0006); for an exam moment, every domain moment; for a pass, the `credential.reported` event.
- **When support fails, the moment goes.** If a supporting answer is disputed or regraded, or a skill is
  added to a domain and is not relearned yet, the moment is recomputed: it moves to the next answer that
  qualifies (a later date), or it leaves the shelf. Any copy shared to a circle is deleted at once, in the
  same request that notices it (a disputed judgement is never read in the learner's favour, and a peer
  must never see a claim the evidence no longer carries). A domain or exam moment that left because a skill
  was added comes back, with the new date, when that skill is relearned too.
- **When a moment needs a refresh.** Time alone never removes or tags a moment (INV-4: no universal expiry
  clock; Know keeps recalls coming anyway). A later miss does not remove it either: the successes it rests
  on still happened. Instead it shows a quiet "Worth a refresh" tag, with the reason in plain words, while
  one of these holds:
  - *skill moments:* an open answer on that skill was judged not yet met after the moment, and no unaided
    full success has followed; or the skill's page changed and its lessons or items were checked again
    after the moment (ADR 0006);
  - *relearned:* also while Know has the skill back on the ladder after a miss and it is not relearned
    again;
  - *domain and exam moments:* while one of their skills' moments needs a refresh.

  The tag goes when the reason is gone: the next unaided full success at least 20 hours after teaching
  (later), the next unaided changed-condition success (new situation), relearned again (relearned and the
  domain and exam above it). A disputed answer never clears a tag; it can only add one. A shared copy
  carries no tag: it is a dated, historical statement, true while its support holds.
- **Where.** The Record (screen 09) gets "Your shelf": moments, newest first, grouped by exam, each with its
  date, what made it, and the line "From Dojo's own questions. Not a certification." (INV-18). Today shows
  a quiet "Your shelf: N moments" link on the Monday after a week that was not met and under the feedback
  of an answer not yet met. That is the "bad week" return path. It never shows a count of anything missing.
- **Private by default.** Nobody else sees the shelf. In team mode, with the social layer on, the learner
  can share one moment at a time to one circle (section 4). Nothing is ever shared automatically.
- **Turning Play on, leaving, and coming back.** Play is off until the learner turns it on. Turning it on,
  the first time or after leaving, creates `learners/<key>/social/play.json` with `since` set to that
  moment, so Play shows only moments, weeks and points from then on. The same screen offers one explicit
  choice, "Include my Record so far", which clears `since` and brings in every moment and week the Record
  already holds; nothing is preselected. "Leave Play" (P-4) deletes everything, `play.json` included, and
  keeps no marker that the learner left: with no `play.json`, Play is simply off, and the next time it is
  turned on `since` is created afresh.

### 2. A weekly rhythm instead of a daily streak (single user; personal layer)

- **Off until the learner turns it on.** "Your week" on Today offers it once and can be dismissed for good.
- **The target.** The learner picks 3, 4, 5 or 6 study days a week (default 3). Which days does not matter.
  A change applies from the next Monday. The week it is turned on needs `ceil(target x days left / 7)`
  days.
- **A day counts** (a **study day**) when it holds at least one retrieval activity from the Record:
  - a judged open answer, written or spoken (probe, practice, check, Know recall, 3-minute check question),
    sent 5 seconds or more after the item was issued;
  - five multiple-choice rehearsal or duel answers, each 5 seconds or more after the previous one;
  - a practice exam closed with at least 10 questions answered (`answered` on `exam.closed`, as Dojo counts
    them: a yes/no statement or a case question each count as one) **and** at least 5 seconds of attempt
    time per answered question (from `exam.started` to `exam.closed`; the same floor as answer points), so
    a rushed exam is not a study day;
  - a DP-800 lab checked after at least one run, whatever the result (not the local demo).

  Lessons, podcasts, coach answers and hints do not count. They are exposure or help, not retrieval
  (I2E; EG-16).
- **The only number is weeks met.** The headline is "Weeks met: N", and this week's "2 of 3 days". It
  never goes down. There is no run, no "in a row" and no freeze: nothing can be lost, so nothing needs
  protecting.
- **No holiday pause.** A pause only made sense to protect a run. Without one, a week away is simply a
  week not met, which changes nothing that is shown. So there is no pause either.
- **Computed from the Record, no ledger.** Weeks met is derived on each request: for every week from the
  week the rhythm was turned on (or `since`, section 1), count the days that hold a retrieval activity and
  compare them with that week's target. The only stored inputs are the target and the dates each target
  took effect. A closed week is never stored, so it cannot drift from the Record, and a dispute or regrade
  can never change a week (a day counts whatever the judgement).
- **What is never shown.** No "you missed", no number of weeks not met, no warning before a week closes, no
  notification. A week not met is followed, on Monday, by the new week and the shelf link (section 1).

### 3. Study points for effort (single user; personal layer)

Points are a mirror of this week's effort. They reward studying on many days, and practice. They are
shown with the rhythm, only to the learner, as this week's total, a breakdown by kind, and the last 8
weeks. There is no lifetime total, because a growing total becomes a level.

| What the Record shows | Points | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **A study day:** the first retrieval activity of the day, exactly as section 2 counts a day (a due recall or any other open answer, written or spoken; five multiple-choice answers; a practice exam with 10 or more answered and at least 5 seconds of attempt time per answer; a lab checked after a run) | 10 | once a day |
| A judged open answer, written or spoken (probe, practice, check, Know recall, 3-minute check question) | 3 | open answers and multiple choice together at most 30 points a day |
| A multiple-choice answer: rehearsal, duel, or practice exam (`exam.answered`, one per question, whatever its ADR 0012 kind) | 1 | (same 30) |
| A lesson read, listened to, presented or watched (`lesson.viewed`) | 2 | 5 views a day |
| A DP-800 lab checked after at least one run in that lab, whatever the result | 10 | once per lab a day; 3 a week |

- **Caps:** 80 points a day and 400 a week. The week resets Monday 00:00 Europe/Berlin.
- **The 5-second floor.** An open answer earns nothing if it was sent less than 5 seconds after its item
  was issued; a rehearsal or duel answer, less than 5 seconds after the previous one. A practice exam's
  answers are written to the Record when it closes, without their own times, so the floor is applied to
  the attempt: it earns at most one point per 5 seconds between `exam.started` and `exam.closed`.
- **Zero points for:** a second judgement of the same item (a regrade or resubmission); a lab in the local
  demo; disputes, hints, coach questions, settings, podcast declarations (a self-report), showing a practice
  exam's rationales, and anything the browser reports that the server did not record. Lesson views are the
  one browser-started event that earns points; they are recorded by the server when a lesson opens and are
  capped at 10 points a day for that reason.
- **No bonus for finishing a practice exam, and none for reviewing it.** An exam's answers earn like any
  multiple-choice answer, under the shared cap; a practice exam with at least 10 answers, and at least 5
  seconds of attempt time per answer, counts as a rhythm
  day (section 2). A completion bonus is an expected reward for finishing a task, the kind that lowers
  free-choice motivation [8], and it paid more for a fast pass through an exam than for the review.
- **The invariant: points depend only on the activities done, never on correctness, and never on the
  schedule a result produced.** No rule reads a judgement, a score, a confidence, a pip or Know's schedule:
  - *per answer:* an open answer earns 3 whether it is met or not; a multiple-choice answer earns 1 whether
    it is right or not; a recall answer earns 3 like any open answer;
  - *per day:* the study day is worth 10 once, for the first retrieval activity of the day, whatever it is
    and however it goes; all answers share the same 30 a day;
  - *over days:* no row asks whether anything was due. A miss makes Know bring a skill back the next day,
    but a due recall earns exactly what any other open answer earns on any day, and the learner can study
    on any day they like. So a miss cannot buy a study day, a point or a more rewarding sequence that a
    success would not equally get.

  The property test in PR 1 pins it: for random event sequences, flipping any judgement (met or not,
  right or wrong, disputed or not) and every schedule that follows from it never changes a day's points
  or the week's.
- **Why the due recall is not paid extra.** An earlier draft paid 10 for a due recall on time. A miss makes
  a skill due sooner, so schedule-driven points let a miss create a more rewarding day. Spacing is still
  rewarded, by the study day: ten points a day for seven days beats one long session, whose answers stop
  at 30. Know's own screens keep showing what is due; Play does not pay for it.
- **Know's words.** The breakdown says "Study days: 3". It never says late, overdue or missed, as ADR 0011
  requires.
- **Computed, never stored.** Points are derived from the Record on each request. The only stored inputs are
  the learner's Play settings. A tampered or missing event can only lower points: an answer with no
  matching `item.issued` earns nothing.
- **Never a level, rank or belt.** The UI says "study points this week".

### 4. Study circles and a shared weekly goal (team mode; social layer)

- **Opt-in, by invitation.** A member who turned on "Others can invite me" can be invited by display name.
  Any member except the owner may start a circle and invite (see "The owner in Play", section 0). An
  invitation is accepted or declined; neither is shown to anyone else, and the inviter never sees pending,
  declined or expired invitations: they see the circle's members, nothing more. An invitation lapses after
  7 days.
- **Size:** 3 to 12 members. A learner can be in at most 3 circles. Three is enough for the witness room
  (sharing a moment or a pass); the shared bar needs more (below). A circle that stays under 3 members for
  7 days is deleted, whether or not invitations are open (as built, after review of PR #44: renewing
  invitations must not keep a small circle forever).
- **Leaving is immediate.** A member's place, invitations and shared copies leave every circle in the same
  locked step as the change that ends them: leaving circles, Play or Dojo, deleting the Record, an admin's
  removal, the membership's expiry, or team mode ending, not at the later purge or the next sweep. Every
  change re-checks the switch and the member's opt-in under the Store lock, so a switch-off or a "Leave
  circles" that happens while a request waits is never undone by it. A shared copy whose support fails is
  deleted by the Record event that took the support away (a dispute, a later answer, a pass reported
  again); each read and the sweep check again (as built, after review of PR #44).
- **The goal.** A weekly target in study points, default and **floor 100 x members**: any member can raise
  next week's goal, never below the floor; the circle sees "Next week's goal: 500", never who changed it.
  The goal is **set when the week starts** (Monday 00:00 Europe/Berlin, from the members counted that
  week) **and fixed for the week**. The floor keeps a band step large: in a circle of 5, one 25% band is at
  least 125 points, more than one member's daily cap of 80, so no single action and no single day of one
  member can cross a band from zero.
- **The bar exists whenever the circle has at least 5 members**, whatever anyone did that week. Its
  presence depends on membership only, never on activity, so it says nothing about who studied. With 3 or
  4 members, subtracting what you know of yourself (and of one friend) from a sum would reveal one person's
  points and active days, so a circle under 5 has **no bar and no "goal met" signal**, only "The bar
  appears when the circle has 5 members." With 5 or more:
  - the bar is **always shown, 0% included**, in **coarse bands only**: 0, 25, 50, 75 or 100% of the goal
    (the sum of members' capped points, rounded down to the band), never a number of points, never anyone's
    share, never a list of who contributed;
  - **a member counts toward the 5, and toward the bar and the goal, only from the Monday after they
    joined** (a lead decision, confirmed in the review of PR 2). Until 5 members count, the circle sees
    "A member counts from the Monday after they join. The bar starts once 5 do." So the bar never appears,
    and never moves, in the middle of a week because someone joined, and a newcomer's first days are never
    shown;
  - it is computed **at most once a day, from events before 00:00** Europe/Berlin, so a change cannot be
    tied to a moment someone was seen studying;
  - when a member leaves, the bar is hidden for the rest of that week (a drop would show what they had
    added).
- **The remaining risk (a lead decision, accepted as residual risk for the privacy reviewers).** A bar
  over 5 or more members does not hide everything. If **every** other member of a circle pools their own
  numbers, they can subtract them from the band and learn that the remaining member studied substantially
  in a period: with the others at zero, a band step means that member earned at least 25 points per
  member of the circle (125 in a circle of 5) by that day. That is effort only, never results: no score,
  answer, skill, pip, moment or confidence can be read from the bar. It needs every other member to
  collude, and the floor and the once-a-day update keep the inference coarse, but it cannot be ruled out.
  Dojo promises no more than this, here, in the notice (P-12) and in `docs/privacy.md`. A learner who does
  not accept it can stay out of circles, or leave one at any time.
- **The shared moment.** When the bar reaches the 100% band, every member sees "Your circle met this
  week's goal." No names. Never shown in a circle under 5.
- **The witness room.** A member may share one of their moments (section 1) or a pass (section 7) to one
  circle. Before sharing, they see exactly what the circle will see (EG-25):
  "<display name> relearned every skill in 'Use GitHub Copilot responsibly' (GH-300) on 2 Oct. From Dojo's
  own questions. Not a certification." The statement is dated and historical, so it stays true when the
  moment later needs a refresh. It is deleted at once if its support fails (section 1). The member can
  withdraw it at any time. A shared item stays in the circle for 4 weeks.
- **The owner in a circle** sees the circle like any member: the bar (under the same 5+ rule) and what
  members chose to share with that circle. Nothing else.

### 5. An optional effort board: weekly bands, no order (team mode; social layer; off by default)

- **Off by default, opt-in per member, and shown only when at least 10 members have joined it.** Below 10
  it says "The board opens when 10 people have joined", with the count. The owner cannot join it (section
  0).
- **What it groups by:** last week's capped study points. Never scores, accuracy, pips, moments, readiness
  or passes.
- **A weekly snapshot, never daily.** Each Monday after 00:00 Europe/Berlin, the board is published once
  from the week that just closed, for the members opted in at that moment. It does not change during the
  week, so it cannot show who studied on which day.
- **Four bands, named for effort:** "Getting going" (0-99), "Building" (100-199), "Steady" (200-299) and
  "Strong week" (300-400). The names describe last week's effort, never the person; none is red, last or
  lowest in the words.
- **What you see:** your own exact points for last week and your band, both read from your Record as it
  was at publication (an answer graded later cannot move the points out of the band). Of the others, only the members in
  **your** band, by display name, in a fixed alphabetical order that says nothing about points, and only
  when at least 3 others share your band; otherwise "You and N others". Nothing about the other bands: no
  names, no counts, no order. No position, no total, no top, no bottom. A member can switch to "just my
  points" at any time.
- **No order, no promotion, no demotion.** Nobody is above or below anyone; a band is a description of one
  week. One board for the whole Dojo, one week at a time.
- **Kept one week.** The snapshot (learner keys, their band and the hash of their last Record event at
  publication; no names, no points) is stored in `play/board.json` and replaced the next Monday. No past week and no history is kept (well
  inside the research's "deleted 4 weeks after a season").
- **Leave any time.** Leaving removes you from the current snapshot at once.
  *As built (PR 3):* a member who joins during the week is on the board from the next Monday; if leaving
  takes this week's board below 10 members, it pauses until the next Monday.
- **Why this is the only form of comparison Dojo offers.** The owner asked for competition or ranking, and the
  research supports some comparison when it is combined with collaboration [1], works like goal-setting
  [11], and gives salient feedback [14]. It also shows what an ordered list does: the top coasts [12], the
  bottom gives up [30], and any order of named colleagues, even a neighbour window, has edges that name the
  top and the bottom. So Dojo offers the one comparison that has no order:
  - it groups *effort* that is capped, so grinding stops paying at 80 a day, and it never looks at
    correctness or the schedule (section 3);
  - it never compares ability, so it cannot announce who is weakest (II.6) and it is not a Belt (II.1);
  - with no order there is no top or bottom, and no edge to reveal one; you learn only who studied about
    as much as you did, which is company, not a verdict ("you are not alone in this band");
  - it is a choice, made after the circles (cooperation) exist, never the default, and leaving costs
    nothing (autonomy, g = 0.64 [3]);
  - the person who runs Dojo is not on it, so it can never become his view of the team.

  It is still a comparison of colleagues' activity. That is why it is off by default, needs 10 people, is
  published once a week, shows names only inside your own band and only among 4 or more, keeps one week,
  and why the social layer exists only behind Team Dojo's written privacy-owner and works-council gate
  (see "Decisions for the owner").

### 6. Duels by invitation (team mode; social layer)

- **Invitation only**, to a member who turned on "Others can invite me" and is enrolled in the same exam.
  The inviter may pick one domain. The owner never sends a duel invitation; he can take part only when a
  member invites him, and wherever he appears in Play (the invite list, the duel, a circle's members) his
  display name carries "runs this Dojo".
- **Ten verified questions.** Dojo writes 10 new single-answer multiple-choice questions with the existing
  rehearsal writer and gate (EG-18; ADR 0012 item types are not used). They never enter either learner's
  practice-exam pool. They are marked seen for a player only when that player opens them: the inviter when
  they start, the invited member only after accepting and opening the duel (ADR 0009 keeps pools per
  learner because peers discuss questions).
- **48 hours, accuracy only.** Each answers in their own time within 48 hours of the invitation. No clock
  and no speed. Times are kept privately, for two purposes only: the invitation time in the duel document
  for expiry, and each answer's time in the player's own Record, like every rehearsal answer. No time is
  ever shown to the opponent or used for the result.
- **The result**, shown only to the two once both have answered all 10: each one's number right. Each
  player then reviews **only their own** misses, privately, with the rationale and the page. There is no
  joint review: a list of questions both missed would tell each player what the other got wrong. No "won",
  no "lost", no record of results.
- **Declining is never recorded.** If the invitation is not accepted within 48 hours, for whatever reason,
  the inviter sees "The duel closed. Here is your result." with their own result only, and nothing about
  the other person. A duel that one player does not finish closes the same way for both.
  *As built (PR 3):* a decline removes the invited member's key from the duel and nothing else, and the
  inviter's view does not change until the 48 hours are over; turning "Others can invite me" off ends
  invitations not yet accepted the same way. Answers given after the 48 hours stay in the player's own
  quick practice but do not count for the duel. A member has at most 3 duels they sent running at once, and
  one at a time with the same person. A duel whose question-writing job is gone (Dojo restarted, or the job
  was lost before it began) is given up after 2 minutes, so it does not hold these limits for a week.
  Leaving Play also deletes a duel the member declined (found by the opaque id it keeps). The person who
  runs this Dojo never gets the list of members who could be invited, since he never invites. The export
  ("Download my record") carries a duel question's answer key, rationale and quote only once the player
  has answered it, so the keys cannot be read before answering.
- **Deleted after 7 days.** The duel object (`play/duels/<id>.json`: the two learner keys, the questions,
  the answers and the result) is deleted 7 days after the invitation, or at once when either leaves Play
  (or circles, their record or Dojo). As built, the answers themselves live only in each player's own quick
  practice; the duel keeps each player's quick-practice id and, once both have finished, the two numbers right.
- **The answers stay in each player's own Record**, as ordinary multiple-choice rehearsal answers in their
  own rehearsal document. This is required, not optional: the rationale after an answer is teaching, and
  leaving it out would make a later check look more "later" than it is (a missing event is never read in
  the learner's favour). These entries name no opponent. They fill no pip.
- **Cost:** writing 10 questions is one rehearsal, counted against the inviter's rehearsal allowance
  (ADR 0009).

### 7. Pass celebrations (team mode for the celebration; the report itself works single user)

- **The learner reports a pass.** "I passed" on the exam's page records `credential.reported` in the
  learner's own Record: `package`, `exam`, `title`, `earned` (the date they give), `source`
  (`self` or `learn_transcript`) and `checked_at`. A pass is an external issuer's award and belongs in the
  Capability Record (II.8, INV-16). The shelf derives an "Exam: passed" moment from it ("You reported
  passing GH-300"). Dojo never asks for the score and never stores one.
- **The celebration** (social layer, opt-in, per pass): the learner may share it to one circle as
  "<display name> reports passing GH-300", the same words when a transcript listing was found. Never a
  score, never a date of the attempt, never the number of tries.
- **Verification through a Microsoft Learn transcript: included as best effort, in its own late PR,
  self-reported by default.**
  - *Why include it.* A credential in the Record should name its issuer and how Dojo knows (INV-17,
    INV-18). A transcript listing is a better anchor than a self-report.
  - *Why best effort.* The address `https://learn.microsoft.com/api/profiles/transcript/share/{code}` is
    undocumented; it comes from a community tool and was not tested with a real code [40]. It may change
    without notice.
  - *How.* The learner pastes their Learn "Share link". Dojo extracts the code with one strict pattern and
    builds the address itself (it never fetches a URL the learner typed). It does one GET: host exactly
    `learn.microsoft.com`, path exactly `/api/profiles/transcript/share/<code>` with `<code>` matching
    `[A-Za-z0-9_-]{8,64}` (narrowed to the real format once one is seen), no query, no cookies, no
    redirects followed, 10-second timeout, at most 512 KB, JSON only. It reads only
    `certificationData.activeCertifications[].name` and `.dateEarned` (EG-37: everything else is ignored),
    keeps the one whose name matches the exam's certification title, and stores only that name and date.
    The link, the code, the response and the transcript owner's name are discarded and never logged.
    At most 3 tries per learner per day.
  - *The narrowest rule.* `app/sources.py` does not change: `ALLOWED_HOSTS` stays the two documentation
    hosts, and `NOT_DOCS` keeps refusing `/api/...` for documentation reads. The one new outbound address
    is a single constant pattern in `app/credentials.py`, and a test pins that nothing else can be fetched
    through it.
  - *The words.* "Listed on a shared Microsoft Learn transcript (checked 2 Oct)" or "Self-reported". Never
    "verified", because Dojo cannot tell whose transcript it was. Before pasting, the page says: turn off
    legal name and email in your transcript settings, and delete the share link in Learn afterwards.
  - *Fallback.* Any failure or no match saves the pass as self-reported, with one calm line.
  - *Peers never see the difference.* The circle sees the same "reports passing GH-300" either way, so
      nobody is pressed to share a transcript.

## What works for the owner alone, today

With team mode off, the personal layer is the whole feature, and it is useful on its own:

- **The shelf** fills at once from his existing Record when he turns Play on and chooses "Include my Record
  so far": every skill that already shows "later" or "new situation" (for example proxy filtering, d3)

The personal layer, circles and the board make no model calls: counting only. A duel costs one rehearsal

## Consequences

- The owner gets the personal layer the day PR 1 deploys; nothing about his evidence, readiness or plan changes.
- The vision's mastery moments exist for the first time, as a shelf.
- Dojo has no score leaderboard and will not get one without a new ADR. The answer to "compete or rank"
  is: cooperate in circles, duel a colleague who accepts, and, if 10 people want it, see who shared your
  band of capped effort last week, with no order.
- Play keeps almost nothing about anyone: moments, weeks met and points are derived from the learner's own
  Record when they look; only settings, deletions, shared copies, a one-week board snapshot and live duels
  are stored.
- The daily banded bar and the weekly board are stale and coarse on purpose.
- Small circles (3 or 4 members) get the witness room but no bar. That is the price of not revealing one
  person's effort by subtraction; at 5 or more the bar is always there, so its presence says nothing.
- The bar's steps are not fully private: if every other member pools their own numbers, they can learn
  that the remaining member studied substantially in a period (effort only, never results). The lead
  accepted this as residual risk; it is put to the privacy reviewers and stated in the notice.
- A domain or exam moment leaves the shelf when Dojo adds a skill to the domain, and comes back when that
  skill is relearned. The shelf describes the evidence as it is, so it can never overclaim.
- Know's due recalls earn no more than any other open answer. Spacing is rewarded by the study day (coming
  back on another day pays; a longer session does not), not by the schedule, so no result can buy points.
- Lesson views earn a few points even though Dojo cannot see whether a lesson was really followed. The cap
  keeps that small.

## Decisions for the owner

1. Switch the social layer on (`play_social`), after team mode is on. Default: off.
2. The gate is Team Dojo's, not a separate question: team mode, and with it Play's social layer, is not
   switched on for real learners until the accountable privacy owner outside the build team and, for
   colleagues in Germany or the Netherlands, the works councils have accepted `docs/privacy.md`, including
   Play's section, in writing (ADR 0009). That section names one residual risk for them to accept or
   reject: the circle bar (section 4). If Play's section is added after that acceptance, it needs a
   fresh acceptance before `play_social` goes on (ADR 0009, review triggers). Default: off until then.
3. Transcript listing: paste one Learn transcript share link (legal name and email turned off) so PR 4 can
   be tested, or skip it. Default: skipped; passes are self-reported.

## Review triggers

- Anyone proposes ranking by scores, accuracy, readiness, pips or uncapped points, a lifetime total, a
  level, or any Play view for the owner, a manager or HR. Rejected unless a new ADR answers every reason in
  option 2.
- Weekly totals cluster at the caps (grinding pays), or nobody reaches half of them (the table is wrong).
- Learners turn "Your week" off within a few weeks, or never share a moment.
- Know changes its ladder, its cap or its fields; Exam realism (ADR 0012) adds more item kinds (each
  `exam.answered` counts as one multiple-choice answer, whatever its kind).
- Members outside the Europe/Berlin zone (the day and week boundaries).
- The transcript address stops working, or Microsoft documents a verification API.
- A works council or privacy reviewer asks for changes, including rejecting the circle bar's residual
  risk (then the bar and the goal-met moment go, and circles keep the witness room).
- A member reports, or a reviewer shows, that circle members pooled their numbers to infer one member's
  effort, or a circle of exactly 5 forms mostly to watch one member: review the bar (raise the minimum,
  coarser bands, or weekly updates only).
- ADR 0013 adds push notifications (only the three in section 0 are allowed).

## How to undo it

Turn `play_social` off: circles, duels and the board disappear; their documents are deleted by the next
sweep. Remove the Today card and the shelf: the personal layer is derived, so nothing needs migrating.
Delete `play/` and every `learners/<key>/social/play.json`. Reported passes and duel answers stay in the learners'
Records, where they belong.
